Medicare Advantage — Part C

All-in-one plans from private insurers

Medicare Advantage plans replace Original Medicare with a single plan from a private, Medicare-approved company. They must cover everything Parts A and B cover — and most add prescription drugs, dental, vision, and hearing, often for a $0 additional premium.

The trade-off: you generally use the plan's network of doctors and hospitals.

Why people choose it

  • ✓Low or $0 additional monthly premium
  • ✓Annual cap on out-of-pocket spending
  • ✓Often includes dental, vision, hearing & fitness
  • ✓Usually bundles Part D drug coverage
  • ✓One card, one plan, one company

What to watch for

  • Provider networks (HMO/PPO) limit your doctors
  • Prior authorization for some services
  • Plans can change benefits every year
  • Out-of-network care can be costly or uncovered

Medicare Supplement — Medigap

Fill the gaps in Original Medicare

Medigap plans work alongside Original Medicare (Parts A and B), paying most of the deductibles and 20% coinsurance Medicare leaves behind. You keep the freedom to see any doctor in the country who accepts Medicare — no networks, no referrals.

Plans are standardized by letter (A through N), so a Plan G from one company covers the same things as a Plan G from another; only the price and service differ.

Why people choose it

  • ✓See any doctor in the U.S. who accepts Medicare
  • ✓Very predictable, low out-of-pocket costs
  • ✓No referrals or prior authorizations
  • ✓Standardized plans (A–N); Plan G is popular
  • ✓Guaranteed renewable for life

What to watch for

  • Higher monthly premium on top of Part B
  • No dental, vision or hearing extras
  • No drug coverage — pair with Part D
  • Medical underwriting may apply after your initial window
